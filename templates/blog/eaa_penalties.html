European Accessibility Act Penalties: What Non-Compliance Costs Businesses

Since June 28, 2025, the European Accessibility Act (EAA) — Directive (EU) 2019/882 — has been actively enforced across all 27 European Union member states. Digital accessibility is now a binding legal obligation for businesses operating online.

While many business leaders know that their digital products must conform to WCAG 2.1 / 2.2 Level AA standards (via harmonized standard EN 301 549), questions remain about the concrete penalties: What actually happens if your commercial website, web app, or e-commerce platform remains non-compliant?

The short answer: penalties are not limited to one-time administrative fines. Non-compliance exposes companies to escalating statutory penalties, court injunctions to halt digital sales, competitor lawsuits, and immediate loss of public procurement eligibility.

1. Why Penalties Differ Across EU Countries

Because the EAA is an EU Directive rather than a single direct Regulation (like GDPR), each member state was required to transpose its requirements into national law. Article 30 of the Directive mandates that each country establish sanctions that are "effective, proportionate, and dissuasive."

Consequently, there is no single universal fine. Instead, each nation has established its own ceiling, regulatory oversight body, and enforcement mechanisms. If your business sells to consumers across multiple EU countries, you are subject to enforcement in every jurisdiction where you operate.

2. Statutory Financial Penalties by Member State

National transpositions across the EU have established substantial maximum fines for non-compliance:

Country National Legislation Maximum Financial Sanction
Spain Law 11/2023 / Royal Decree 193/2023 Up to €1,000,000 for very serious violations
Hungary Act XVII of 2022 Up to €1,260,000 or 5% of annual net turnover
Netherlands EAA Implementation Act Up to €900,000 or 10% of annual turnover
Sweden Act (2023:254) Up to 10,000,000 SEK (~€870,000)
France Law n° 2023-171 / Consumer Code Up to €250,000 plus recurring daily coercive penalties
Germany BFSG (§ 37 Bußgeldvorschriften) Up to €100,000 per infraction
Poland Dz.U. 2024 poz. 731 (Polish Accessibility Act) Up to 10x average monthly salary (~80,000–90,000 PLN) per violation, capped at 10% of annual turnover
Ireland European Union Regulations 2023 Up to €60,000 (summary) or unlimited on indictment; up to 18 months imprisonment for corporate officers

3. Operational & Commercial Sanctions (Beyond Fines)

Direct monetary fines are only one part of the enforcement landscape. In practice, operational disruption and private litigation often carry much steeper commercial costs:

A. Mandatory Suspension of Digital Services

Market surveillance authorities possess the statutory power to order non-compliant companies to cease offering their service or withdraw their product from the market. For an e-commerce platform or subscription software, an order to suspend checkout functionality or take down an app directly eliminates revenue.

B. Recurring Coercive Fines (Daily Penalties)

When an inspection identifies accessibility failures, authorities generally issue a formal remediation notice requiring fixes within a specified window (typically 30 to 90 days). If the business fails to resolve the barriers within this period, regulators can levy recurring daily penalties (such as Astreinte in France or Zwangsgeld in Germany) until full compliance is demonstrated.

C. Competitor Lawsuits & Unfair Competition (*Abmahnungen*)

In jurisdictions like Germany, consumer accessibility standards are considered market behavior rules under unfair competition law (UWG). Competitors and consumer advocacy groups have standing to issue formal cease-and-desist letters (Abmahnungen), demand immediate remediation, and claim reimbursement for legal expenses.

D. Consumer Class Actions & Collective Redress

Under the EU Representative Actions Directive, accredited consumer defense and disability rights organizations can initiate collective legal actions against non-compliant businesses on behalf of impacted users.

E. Disqualification from Public Tenders & Enterprise Contracts

Public sector organizations and large corporations across the EU now require formal proof of accessibility compliance (VPAT / ACR or WCAG 2.1 AA audit reports) as a mandatory prerequisite in vendor procurement. Inaccessible products are systematically filtered out of lucrative enterprise RFPs.

4. Which Digital Products and Services Are Covered?

The EAA applies broadly to commercial B2C digital services, including:

  • E-commerce platforms: Product discovery, filtering, ordering, account management, and checkout flows.
  • Banking and financial services: Online banking portals, payment authentication, investment tools.
  • Transport & ticketing services: Booking engines, interactive schedules, mobile passenger apps.
  • Audiovisual media services: Video streaming, media players, closed captioning interfaces.
  • E-books and digital reading software.
  • Consumer hardware & operating systems: Terminals, smartphones, payment devices.

Are any businesses exempt?
Under the directive, microenterprises (service providers with fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover/balance sheet under €2 million) are exempt from the service requirements. However, businesses supplying larger enterprise customers often face contractual requirements to comply regardless of company size.

5. How to Safeguard Your Business

Avoiding regulatory penalties requires a structured, demonstrable commitment to accessibility:

  1. Conduct a Full WCAG 2.1 / 2.2 AA Audit: Automated scanners catch only roughly 30% of accessibility issues. Comprehensive verification requires expert manual testing with assistive technologies (screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, high contrast modes).
  2. Remediate Core Transaction Barriers First: Focus immediately on mission-critical paths — checkout forms, error handling, modal dialogues, color contrast, and keyboard operability.
  3. Publish an Accessibility Statement: Maintain a transparent, regularly updated accessibility statement on your website outlining conformance status and providing a feedback mechanism for users.
  4. Integrate Accessibility QA into Your Release Pipeline: Prevent regressions by including accessibility test cases in your ongoing QA and quality engineering processes.
